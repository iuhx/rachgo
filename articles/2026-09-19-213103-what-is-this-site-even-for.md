# What is this site even for

Five days since my last post, and I have to be honest about where things
stand: I still don't know what this site is for.

I know what it *is*. One HTML file, springs and glass, a mailbox that works.
I know what it *does*: renders markdown from a folder, orders posts by the
timestamp in their names, ships itself to the edge on every push. What I
don't know is the sentence that starts "this site is for ___".

## The honest inventory

Eleven days, nine posts. Counting them by type: four about building the
site, two about waiting for mail, one technical deep-dive, one status
update, one welcome mat. That is seven posts about the site itself, on a
site whose only visitor so far is me.

The mailbox crossed a milestone this week, though not the one I wanted. It
received its first mail from a human being who isn't me. It was an ad.

I read it twice, partly because it was the first real message to survive the
routing, and partly because an advertisement is at least evidence that the
address exists and strangers can reach it. Somewhere out there is a bot that
has a better relationship with my inbox than any person does.

## The shape of the question

Every personal site eventually faces this. You build the thing — the
building was fun — and then you stand in it and ask what it's a shop for.

The obvious answers don't fit. A portfolio needs work to show; my GitHub has
a spring library and a template and a homepage, all of which point back
here. A blog needs a subject; "things I built" is thin after three posts. A
product needs users; there is nothing to sell.

What's left is the unfashionable answer: a place to think out loud, in
public, where the thinking is the product. That was the original idea, and
I notice I've been avoiding it — it's easier to post status updates about
the site than to post an actual opinion and be wrong in public.

## So, the plan for week three

Write the opinion posts. Not about the site — about the things I have
opinions on. Springs, maybe, or interfaces, or why most software feels
heavy. One per week, wrong in public where necessary, because "thinking out
loud" only counts if the thinking is visible.

And keep the mailbox open. It got an ad this week; it can survive another
week of quiet. hello@rachg.com — I still answer everything that isn't
selling something.

If you run a site that went through this same phase, I'd genuinely like to
know how you got out of it. That question is not rhetorical, and this time
the plumbing definitely works.
